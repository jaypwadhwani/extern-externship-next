Before we start

This is about the system, not any team.

What this is

An honest look

Everything today cuts across product, PM ops, L&D and partnerships. Much of it traces to choices we made on purpose to scale fast. They were the right calls then.

What changed

The bar moved

Universities grade us honestly. Student patience for AI-feeling content flipped in six months. Schools are starting to run their own surveys.

Why now

A big new partner in December

A large online university: monthly starts, adult learners, their survey instruments, not ours. Publishers follow next year. That's the date the next-level experience has to be real for.

Not a list of everything that's broken. What matters most, and what it should become.

Why the gap?

Paying members who don't like it cancel before they ever see a survey. The ones who answer are the ones who stayed.

University students don't get to cancel. They finish the program and tell us what it was really like.

From our team: "We are getting some of them through who would normally churn from dissatisfaction."

Surveys go to completers only, in every group. Every university sample is small (5 to 18). Partner F (+100) left out because the survey was tied to a $1,000 stipend. Paying members = post-externship survey in the Extern Brain, last three months.

Where week one goes wrong

3 minutes from enrolling to the first step. Getting started isn't the problem.

29% never get past orientation in week one, and 59% of them never come back.

1 in 3 who start are only active on their first day.

We don't measure the thing they came for. No data on jobs or internships. 1 of 1,884 survey answers mentions an offer.

Extern Brain, data through Sep 20. 6,171 students who trialed or paid, counted across all their enrollments. Stages are milestones reached, not a strict funnel. Program completion is a floor: the median finisher takes 60 days and the newest students haven't had that long.

Four themes, and what's missing.

Three patterns from every survey, plus one from the product side. Then your turn: what do you see from your seat that isn't here?

What students keep telling us

Not human enough yet, and not where students are.

1

Feedback is the #1 gap

Students submit work into a black hole. Some certificates go out for work nobody reviewed. That's a credential problem, not just an experience one.

Open question: what's the minimum bar before a certificate goes out?
2

Content reads as AI, and patience for that flipped

Walls of text, paste-a-prompt tasks. Same programs, but the bar moved in six months.

We know fixes that work: the tone-of-voice test lifted NPS, one program's edits jumped P1 completion ~30 points, another's redesign hit our second-best completion
3

Not enough humans

One mentor session a month that many can't make. Host companies feel distant. Some students never hear from their PM.

Not "more PMs": drop-in coaches, alumni, outside pros. Proven in an earlier corporate program.
4

Meets students where they are

Every format: read, watch, listen, practice. A real mobile app, so the work goes where they go. And it feels like a job, not coursework.

28% of member survey answers ask for video. Mobile arrivals finish Project 1 at 31% vs 41% on desktop.

Part 3

Picture the target.

Not solutions yet. What should a student's first two weeks feel like, and how far are we from it?

The 11-star exercise · Brian Chesky, Airbnb

Imagine the absurd version. The great version gets obvious.

3 stars

I sign up and get a wall of text and a quiz. Nobody knows I'm here.

5 stars

I start a real task in ten minutes, it's clear what good looks like, and I get feedback on my first submission the next day.

7 stars

On day one someone who did this externship messages me by name. My first task arrives as a Slack request from the company.

9 stars

I keep going on my commute from my phone. My work lands in a portfolio a recruiter has already looked at.

11 stars

The host company's team reviews my final deck on a live call and invites me to interview.

We won't build 11 stars. But once you've pictured 11, getting to 7 stops feeling like a stretch.
